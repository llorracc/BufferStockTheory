## 0. The theorem in plain economics (a reader's guide)

**The question.** A very wealthy consumer facing labor-income risk behaves almost like a
perfect-foresight consumer: income risk is trivial relative to wealth, so the precautionary
motive nearly vanishes. *How fast* does it vanish as wealth grows? That rate matters
computationally — every solution algorithm must assume something above its grid top — and
theoretically: the buffer-stock canon proves the *ratio* version of the approach ([BST's
limiting MPC bounds](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#lemma-mpcboundsconvg))
but neither the level nor the rate (§1.3).

**The answer in one sentence.** Precautionary saving — the shortfall of consumption below
the perfect-foresight rule — dies off as a POWER of wealth ([](#eq-powerlaw), proved in
§§3–4: [](#thm-I), [](#thm-gT)) — the same functional family as the Pareto laws economists
know from wealth, firm-size, and city-size distributions (Gabaix 1999, 2009, 2016) — with an
exponent $\min(1, \qup)$ built from patience, returns, and growth ([](#eq-qstar-def)), and
(when $\qup > 1$) a level $B$ ([](#thm-I), [](#eq-boundary-value)) that is nothing more
exotic than a growing-perpetuity (Gordon) value.

**The asset-pricing reading (the single most useful translation).** Subtract the
perfect-foresight Euler equation from the true one and what remains is, exactly, an
asset-pricing recursion for the shortfall:

$$
\text{shortfall today} \;=\; \text{discount} \times \E[\,\text{shortfall tomorrow}\,] \;+\; \text{this quarter's premium}
$$ (eq-pricing-recursion)

— price equals discounted continuation value plus dividend. (The schematic is made exact in
Lemma 5.1, [](#eq-52); the [translation box](#box-kpp) at the end of §2 spells out the
precise discount and premium.) The "asset" is **precautionary saving** itself —
$s(m) := \bar{c}(m) - c(m)$, consumption's shortfall below the perfect-foresight rule
$\bar{c}$; its "dividend" is this quarter's Kimball precautionary premium
($\approx \kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2/2\wbar$ with $\wbar$ total wealth (§1.1) — an
Arrow–Pratt-type premium: absolute risk over wealth, hence tiny for the rich; [](#eq-54),
§1.4); the discounting per step is $1/\Rcal$, with the state drifting down to $\ThornG \wbar$
(the wealth recursion [](#eq-31)). Everything in the theorem is bookkeeping on this
recursion: the *exponent* is about which dividends dominate the present value (near ones or
far ones), the *amplitude* $B$ is a perpetuity value, and the *trichotomy* is Gordon-growth's
convergent / $r = g$ / divergent trichotomy.

(sec-qstar-intuition)=
### What is $\qup$? (and why $\min(1, \qup)$)

Two per-quarter log-rates, both familiar:

- $\ln \Rcal = \ln R - \ln \Gamma$ — the **interest-minus-growth gap**: how fast discounting
  compounds along the journey;
- $\Lambda = \ln(1/\ThornG)$ — the **impatience gap**: how fast an arbitrarily rich
  household's normalized wealth *descends* toward its buffer-stock target (under GIC, about
  $\Lambda$ percent per quarter).

Their ratio is the star of the theorem (at $\psi\equiv1$; in general the root of [](#eq-E)):

$$
\qup \;=\; \frac{\ln \Rcal}{\Lambda} \;=\; \frac{\text{discounting speed}}{\text{descent speed}}.
$$ (eq-qstar-def)

*The intuition.* The descent from wealth $\wbar$ back to the target takes
$T(\wbar) \approx \ln \wbar / \Lambda$ quarters — time-to-target is LOGARITHMIC in wealth
(made exact by §3.2's shells and [](#thm-gR)'s shell-to-wealth conversion).
("The target" is BST's [buffer-stock target](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#thm-target)
$\hat{m}$; §6 defines the precise [return region around the target](#def-return-region) that
the descent argument uses — the intuition being that bounded shocks confine the recurrent
range of wealth to a compact interval around $\hat{m}$ with a computable upper edge — and
[](#sec-dualroot) quantifies how rarely it is visited.) Anything discounted exponentially in
TIME therefore becomes a power law in WEALTH:

$$
\Rcal^{-T(\wbar)} = \wbar^{-\ln \Rcal/\Lambda} = \wbar^{-\qup}.
$$ (eq-logclock)

**A power law in wealth is exponential discounting read on a logarithmic clock.** (The same
log-clock conversion generates the Pareto tails of the random-growth literature — there
applied to cross-sections of cities and firms, here to a single household's policy function.)
So $\qup$ answers: *by the time the big precautionary action arrives — near the target —
how discounted is it, per unit of log-wealth?*

- $\qup > 1$ (discounting beats the descent): faraway dividends are negligible; the
  shortfall is dominated by the premium collected **here and now**, which is $\propto 1/\wbar$
  (Arrow–Pratt). Realized exponent: $1$ ([](#thm-I)).
- $\qup < 1$ (descent beats discounting): the shortfall is dominated by the large premia
  awaiting **near the target**, discounted back. Realized exponent: $\qup$ ([](#thm-gC)).
- Hence $q = \min(1, \qup)$: the shortfall is a sum of both components, and the
  slower-fading one wins asymptotically ([](#thm-gT)). And it can never fade faster than
  $1/\wbar$ — a prudent consumer is always at least collecting *today's* premium (the
  $1/\wbar$ floor: the lower bound on the forcing under [](#eq-52)). The corollary that this
  floor makes exponential decay *impossible* is a legacy-motivated aside, developed in the
  [appendix](#prop-imp-A0), not in the main line of the theorem.
- Permanent shocks make the descent NOISY (a random-walk component in log wealth). Noise
  lets lucky households linger at high wealth, weakening the effective discounting and
  LOWERING $\qup$ below the deterministic ratio (§5, [](#thm-III); the general-$\psi$ root
  solves [](#eq-E)): for the estimated high-school calibration (CAL-HS, §1.1),
  $\ln \Rcal/\Lambda = 0.43$ but $\qup = 0.38$; at the college patience ceiling the
  deterministic ratio is $\approx 14$, yet $\qup = 1.47$.

:::{note} Notation
$\qup$ (up-arrow: the upper-wealth end; code and the wider literature write $q^{*}$, and
HARK's API is unchanged) is the model's characteristic root; $q = \min(1, \qup)$ is the realized
decay exponent; $\qtilde$ denotes a *trial* exponent in the detection argument of §4;
$s(m) := \bar{c}(m) - c(m)$ is precautionary saving (defined with the gap in §1.1:
$s(m) = g(\wbar)$).
:::

**A characteristic root, and a saddle path.** Economists solve linear rational-expectations
models by finding the characteristic roots and discarding explosive solutions
(Blanchard–Kahn). Equation [](#eq-E) is exactly the characteristic equation of the linearized
Euler dynamics in log wealth: the homogeneous solutions of the shortfall recursion are the
powers $\wbar^{-q}$ with $q$ solving [](#eq-E), and the exponent-detection theorem of §4
([](#thm-gT)) is the corresponding stability selection — compensate by any wrong exponent and
the transformed problem explodes or dies; only $\min(1, \qup)$ is the saddle path.

**Vocabulary.** Where this document says **"the estimated HAFiscal calibrations"** it means
the parameter values estimated by and used in the HAFiscal paper; §1.1 names the three used
here (CAL-HS, CAL-CTOP, CAL-CCAP).

## 1. What is proven, and where it sits in the economics literature

### 1.1 The claim

An infinite-horizon consumer with CRRA utility (coefficient $\rho$) faces the Friedman–Muth
income process of the buffer-stock literature: i.i.d. mean-one permanent shocks $\psi$ and
transitory shocks $\theta$, whose specification includes a zero-income event of probability
$\pZero > 0$ — the atom is part of the maintained income-process assumption (BST,
[Assumption *ass-shocks*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-shocks);
[§2](#sec-model) states the process in full and records exactly where the atom is
load-bearing). Under the buffer-stock conditions — **GIC** (growth impatience,
$\ThornG := (\beta R)^{1/\rho}/\Gamma < 1$,
[BST's *GIC-Raw*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#gicraw) on the growth
patience factor), **RIC** ([return impatience](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-ric),
$\ThornR := (\beta R)^{1/\rho}/R < 1$), and **FHWC**
([finite human wealth](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-fhwc),
$\Rcal := R/\Gamma > 1$) — the normalized consumption function $c(m)$ approaches its
perfect-foresight asymptote from below:

$$
\bar{c}(m) = \kap\,(m - 1 + h), \qquad \kap = 1 - \ThornR \ \text{(BST's limiting MPC)}, \qquad
h = \frac{1}{1 - \Rcal^{-1}} = \frac{R}{R - \Gamma},
$$ (eq-pf-asymptote)

where $h$ is [BST's human wealth](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-hdef)
— the PDV of labor income *including* the current period's unit of it, which is why the rule
([BST's own](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-cfuncpfunc), with
$\kap$ from [its limiting-MPC definition](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-mpcmindef))
carries the $-1$. **The theorem** is about the *rate and form* of that approach. Write

$$
\wbar := b + h = m - 1 + h \qquad \text{(perfect-foresight total wealth, human and market)}
$$ (eq-wbar-def)

— the wealth of the **optimist**, viewed at the decision moment after this period's returns
have been realized on the kapital saved last period: $b$ is BST's bank balances ($b = \Rcal a$,
i.e. $b = m - 1$, market resources net of the unit of current income that $h$ already counts —
[BST's own decomposition](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#mpcmindefn)).
The bar follows the convention BST itself states for $\bar{c}$ — "the overbar signifies that
$\bar{c}$ will be an upper bound as we modify the problem to incorporate constraints and
uncertainty; analogously, $\kap$ is the MPC's lower bound" — and the Method of Moderation
convention (bar = the optimist, underline = the pessimist, matching $\kap$); $w$ itself is
unused in BST, so no clash. In this variable the PF rule is simply $\bar{c} = \kap\,\wbar$,
and

$$
g(\wbar) := \kap\, \wbar - c(m) \;\geq\; 0 \qquad \text{(the gap)},
$$ (eq-gap-def)

which is **precautionary saving** expressed in $\wbar$: $s(m) := \bar{c}(m) - c(m) = g(\wbar)$.
The gap decays as a **power law with exponent $\min(1, \qup)$**,

$$
g(\wbar) \asymp \wbar^{-\min(1,\, \qup)} \qquad (\times \ln \wbar \ \text{exactly at the knife-edge } \qup = 1),
$$ (eq-powerlaw)

where $\qup$ is the unique positive root of the eigen-equation

$$
\E[\psi^{1+q}] = \Rcal\, \ThornG^{\,q}
\qquad\Bigl(\psi \equiv 1:\ \ \qup = \frac{\ln \Rcal}{\Lambda},\quad \Lambda := \ln(1/\ThornG)\Bigr),
$$ (eq-E)

with sharp closed-form amplitudes. At $\qup > 1$,

$$
\wbar\, g(\wbar) \to B = \frac{\kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2}{2(\Rcal\ThornG - 1)}
\qquad \text{(transitory-only; } \sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}\theta\text{)},
$$ (eq-B)

$$
\wbar\, g(\wbar) \to B_\psi = \frac{\kap(\rho+1)\sigma_B^2}{2(\Rcal\ThornG - \E[\psi^2])}
\qquad \text{(permanent shocks; } \sigma_B^2 \text{ below)},
$$ (eq-Bpsi)

and at $\qup = 1$, $(\wbar/\ln \wbar)\, g(\wbar) \to \kap(\rho+1)\sigma_B^2 / (2\,\E[\psi^2]\,\Lfun'(1))$
(at $\psi\equiv 1$: $\kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2/(2\Lambda)$; [](#eq-resonance-limit)). At
$\qup < 1$ the sharp form carries a periodic factor $P$ (Theorems A2/B2 of the original
chain; the envelope is [](#thm-gC)). [](#fig-gap) shows the object and the three slopes.

### 1.2 Situating the result: function-approach, not distribution-tail

A reader who knows the modern heavy-tails literature in economics should be told immediately
what this theorem is *not*. The celebrated power laws of Toda (2014),
Benhabib–Bisin–Zhu (2011, 2015), Stachurski–Toda (2019), Beare–Toda (2022), and
Gouin-Bonenfant–Toda (2023) concern the **cross-sectional distribution of the level of
wealth**: Pareto tails of a *stationary distribution*, with the tail exponent solving a
Kesten-type root equation for the *forward* dynamics. **This paper's object is different in
kind**: it is the **consumption function itself** — a policy function, not a distribution —
and the claim is that $c(m)$ approaches the perfect-foresight solution according to a
power-law *decay process in the individual decision-maker's wealth* as that wealth goes to
infinity.

The distinction is sharp, not cosmetic. In the transitory-only model ($\psi \equiv 1$,
bounded $\theta$) under GIC, the ergodic distribution of wealth has **compact support** —
there is *no* Pareto tail of any kind, a fact whose econ-packaged form is precisely
Stachurski–Toda's (2019) impossibility theorem. Yet the consumption gap is *exactly* a power
law. Formally, the two literatures' root equations are **Mellin duals**: the
distributional-tail equation $\E[(\ThornG/\psi)^\zeta] = 1$ may fail to have any root (it
does fail at $\psi \equiv 1$) while the primal equation [](#eq-E) always has one — the level
$\Rcal > 1$ is what saves it. The full kinship map is `alt_proof_econlit.md` §6. Section
[](#sec-dualroot) develops the dual root explicitly — with the estimated calibrations' numbers — for readers
meeting it for the first time.

the model *with* the atom routes through BST's

machinery ([§2](#sec-model)).

### 1.4 The forcing is a precautionary premium

The engine below runs on a one-step recursion whose inhomogeneous term $J(\wbar)$ is, to leading
order, $\kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2/(2\ThornG) \cdot \wbar^{-1}$ — economically, **($\kap$ times) the
Kimball (1990) equivalent precautionary premium** of the coming period's normalized income
noise at wealth scale $\wbar$: the premium form $\psi_K \approx \eta\,\sigma^2/2$ with CRRA
relative prudence $\eta \cdot c = \rho+1$. Its *sign* (uncertainty depresses consumption below
the PF line) is Leland (1968)/Sandmo (1970); its small-risk quadratic template is Pratt
(1964); its consumption interpretation is Drèze–Modigliani (1972).

:::{note} Precision note (it trips auditors)
Kimball (1990) states the *premium* form with coefficient $\rho+1$; the *multiplicative*
expansion $\E[u'(c+W)] = u'(c)\bigl(1 + \tfrac{\rho(\rho+1)}{2}\operatorname{Var}(W)/c^2 +
\dots\bigr)$ carries $\rho(\rho+1)$, and both appear below — the Taylor constants $c_2, c_2'$
carry $\rho(\rho+1)/2$ while the envelope algebra reduces the forcing limit to $(\rho+1)$.
`alt_proof_econlit.md` §3.2 reconciles the bookkeeping line by line.
:::

## 2. Model, conditions, and the imported foundations

(sec-model)=
**The model in full** (each element deep-linked to its anchor in the
[published BST draft](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/); the statement is
BST's, so a reader can check any piece against the source).

*The symbol correspondence.* This corpus (and its HARK consumers) writes relative risk
aversion as $\rho$ and permanent income growth as $\Gamma$; BST's published draft writes
$\gamma$ and $\mathcal{G}$ for the same objects. Everything else is shared:
$\Thorn := (R\beta)^{1/\rho}$ (the [absolute patience factor](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#apfac)),
$\Rcal := R/\Gamma$, $\kap$ and $\bar{\kappa}$ (the limiting MPC bounds), $\pZero$ (the
zero-income probability), $\psi$ and $\theta$ (permanent and transitory shocks). One
structural note: BST separates the transitory shock into the zero-income atom and the
employed-state shock ($\xi$ vs $\theta$ there); this corpus writes the all-in transitory
shock as the single symbol $\theta$ — so the corpus's $\theta$ is BST's $\xi$, and
$\sigma^2 := \operatorname{Var}\theta$ includes the atom.

*Preferences.* CRRA period utility $u(c) = c^{1-\rho}/(1-\rho)$ with discount factor
$\beta > 0$ (BST, [Setup](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#subsec-setup)).
BST maintains $\rho > 1$, and the existence routing below inherits that; the decay arguments
of §§3–5 use only $\rho > 0$ once existence is in hand (assumption (A1) below).

*The income process* (Friedman–Muth; BST,
[Assumption *ass-shocks*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-shocks),
with the atom displayed at [its transitory-shock definition](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-transhkdef)).
Permanent shocks $\psi$: $\E[\psi] = 1$, supported on $[\psi_{\min}, \psi_{\max}] \subset
(0, \infty)$. Transitory shocks: income is zero with probability $\pZero > 0$ — the
zero-income event, **part of the assumption itself**, not an optional garnish — and
otherwise equals the employed-state shock (mean one, supported on a compact interval bounded
away from zero, per BST) scaled by $1/(1-\pZero)$; the all-in shock $\theta$ then satisfies
$\E[\theta] = 1$ and $0 \leq \theta \leq \theta_{\max} < \infty$. **Both shocks are bounded
and mean-one in levels** — this general statement, BST's own (Assumption
[*ass-shocks*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-shocks)'s
bounded-support requirement), is what every theory section below argues from; the
equiprobable discretizations of lognormal shocks used by the figures are implementations
consistent with it (bounded support excludes unbounded specifications but is consistent with
all standard discretizations of unbounded distributions onto finite grids). One clause on
what the atom buys: the results here do not all *need* $\pZero > 0$ — with
$\theta_{\min} > 0$ existence routes through MST (2020) instead (below) — but $\pZero > 0$
is BST's maintained specification, it is what the estimated calibrations use, and it is
load-bearing for the form of WRIC, for the
natural borrowing constraint (the discussion following
[Assumption *ass-shocks*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-shocks)),
and for the MPC upper bound $\bar{\kappa} = 1 - \pZero^{1/\rho}\ThornR$.

*Budget transitions and normalization.* Normalizing by permanent income, the problem is
[BST's normalized Bellman equation](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-veqnnrmrecbellman):
choose $0 < c < m$ (as in BST's display) with transitions

$$
a = m - c, \qquad b' = \bigl(R/(\Gamma\psi')\bigr)\,a, \qquad m' = b' + \text{(transitory income)}',
$$

where $a$ is end-of-period kapital and $b'$ next period's bank balances — the returns
realized on the kapital saved, the market-wealth leg of $\wbar$ ([](#eq-wbar-def)). The
first-order condition is
[BST's normalized Euler equation](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-scaledeuler),
$c^{-\rho} = \beta R\,\E[(\Gamma\psi')^{-\rho} c(m')^{-\rho}]$. At $\psi \equiv 1$ the
growth-adjusted return is the constant $\Rcal = R/\Gamma$ and $b' = \Rcal a$.

*Human wealth and the perfect-foresight benchmark.* Normalized human wealth $h$ —
[BST's *hNrm*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-hdef), the PDV of
labor income *including* the current period's — has infinite-horizon limit
$h = 1 + \Rcal^{-1} + \Rcal^{-2} + \cdots = 1/(1-\Rcal^{-1}) = R/(R-\Gamma)$ under FHWC (one
line from BST's finite-horizon definition).
[BST's perfect-foresight consumption function](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-cfuncpfunc)
is $\bar{c}(m) = (m - 1 + h)\,\kap$ with limiting MPC
[$\kap := 1 - \ThornR$](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-mpcmindef);
its [finite-horizon form](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#mpcmindefn)
displays BST's own decomposition $b := m - 1$ (bank balances), so
$\bar{c} = (b + h)\kap = \kap\,\wbar$ in §1.1's variable, and precautionary saving is
$s(m) := \bar{c}(m) - c(m) = \kap\wbar - c(m) = g(\wbar)$ — the object of the theorem. The
one bookkeeping trap of the correspondence: $h$'s leading term *is* this period's income, so
the PF rule carries the $-1$; dropping it shifts $g$ by $\kap$ (checked to machine
precision).

Formally, the sandwich [](#eq-five-up)–[](#eq-five-dn) is a *pricing* recursion — shortfall =

(box-kpp)=
:::{admonition} Translation box — is $s_t = \beta\,\E_t[s_{t+1}] + \text{KPP}_t$ exact?
:class: note
Write precautionary saving $s_t(m) := \bar{c}_t(m) - c_t(m)$; in the limiting problem
$s(m) = g(\wbar)$ (§1.1). The exact statement the proof uses is Lemma 5.1, [](#eq-52): for
$\wbar \geq \wbar_0$,

$$
(1 + r_L(\wbar))\,\Rcal\, s \;=\; (1 + r_R(\wbar))\,\E[\,s'\,] \;+\; J(\wbar),
$$

i.e. *precautionary saving today $= \Rcal^{-1} \times$ E[precautionary saving tomorrow]
$+\ \Rcal^{-1} \times$ this period's premium $J$* — **exactly**, up to the displayed weights
$1 + r$, each within $K/\wbar$ of one. So: is the literal
$s_t = \beta\,\E_t[s_{t+1}] + \text{KPP}_t$ (KPP = the Kimball prudence premium) exact?
**No — but it is the correct reading to second order**, and the exact statement differs in
three places. (i) The discount is the growth-adjusted return $\Rcal^{-1} = \Gamma/R$, not
$\beta$. (ii) Naively subtracting the two Euler equations forgets that today's saving is
*invested* — the $\Rcal\,g(\wbar)$ term in the wealth recursion [](#eq-31) — a budget
feedback the exact weights carry. (iii) The dividend is the exact Jensen gap $J(\wbar)$ in
marginal utility, which coincides with the Kimball premium only to second order. To leading
order the two $\beta$-corrections factor the exact discount: converting $\beta$ through CRRA
marginal-utility curvature along the perfect-foresight path turns it into $1/\ThornG$ (since
$\beta R \Gamma^{-\rho}\,\ThornG^{-(\rho+1)} = 1/\ThornG$, using $\Thorn = (R\beta)^{1/\rho}$),
and the invested-saving feedback contributes the return patience factor $\ThornR$; their
product is $\ThornR/\ThornG = \Rcal^{-1}$ — the exact lemma needs neither approximation.
Finally, the KPP itself: by [](#eq-54), $\wbar\,J(\wbar) \to c_J = \kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2/(2\ThornG)$,
and the $(\rho+1)$ is CRRA **relative prudence**
([Kimball 1990](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#cite-kimball-smallandlarge))
— so per unit of next-period wealth the dividend *is* the Kimball precautionary premium to
second order, $J \asymp \sigma^2/\wbar$, with the exact $J$ differing at higher order (the
weights carry relative error $O(1/\wbar)$).

### 3.1 The coordinate, and "assuming the power law in the limit" as a choice of units

Map $z := 1/\wbar$. The half-line $[\wbar_0, \infty)$ becomes $(0, \bar{z}]$, and **infinite
wealth becomes the finite boundary point $z = 0$** — adjoin it. (This is the one-dimensional
Poincaré-compactification chart of dynamical systems; Dumortier–Llibre–Artés 2006, ch. 5 —
decorative ancestry, no theorem of theirs is consumed.) Then choose units that *embed the
conjectured power law*: the **compensated gap**

$$
W_{\qtilde}(z) := \wbar^{\qtilde}\, g(\wbar), \qquad \qtilde = \text{the compensation (trial) exponent}.
$$ (eq-compensated-gap)

"Assume the power law holds in the limit" is thereby demoted from a hypothesis to a **choice
of coordinates**: nothing is assumed — §4's Theorem II shows the compactified problem itself
*detects* the right exponent. [](#fig-schematic) gives the geometry.

### 3.2 The shell equation

Partition $(0, \bar{z}]$ into geometric **shells** $S_n$ (log-width $\Lambda$), accumulating
at the boundary; enlarge each by the constant $\zeta := C_0/(1-\ThornG)$ — the exact fixed
point of the one-step displacement map (the unique $\zeta$ with $\ThornG\zeta + C_0 = \zeta$)
— so the referred point of every $\wbar$ in shell $n{+}1$ lands **exactly** in shell $n$
(Lemma γ0: no band slack, no self-reference). In these units the imported sandwich
[](#eq-five-up)–[](#eq-five-dn) becomes the **shell equation** (Lemma γ1).

(eq-gup)=
For compensation $\qtilde$, its upper and lower branches read

$$
W_{\qtilde}(\wbar) \leq \Bigl(1 + \tfrac{\hat{D}}{\wbar}\Bigr)\bigl[\, \lambda(\qtilde)\, W_{\qtilde}(\ThornG \wbar - C_0) + F_{\qtilde}(\wbar) \,\bigr]
$$ (eq-gamma-up)

$$
W_{\qtilde}(\wbar) \geq \Bigl(1 - \tfrac{\hat{D}}{\wbar}\Bigr)\bigl[\, \lambda(\qtilde)\, W_{\qtilde}(\ThornG \wbar + C_0) + F_{\qtilde}(\wbar) \,\bigr],
$$ (eq-gamma-dn)

with **boundary-continuous coefficients**: the multiplier $\lambda(\qtilde) = \ThornG^{\,\qup - \qtilde}$
(at $\psi \equiv 1$ — an algebraic identity via $\Rcal = \ThornG^{-\qup}$) and the forcing
$F_{\qtilde}(\wbar) = \wbar^{\qtilde} J(\wbar)/\Rcal \cdot (1 + O(1/\wbar))$, whose boundary
limit at $\qtilde = 1$ is $F(0) := c_J/\Rcal$ by [](#eq-54), consumed *rate-free*. There is
also the exact identity

$$
(1 + r_L)\,\Rcal\, W_1(\wbar) = (1 + r_R)\,\E\Bigl[\tfrac{\wbar}{\wbar'}\, W_1(\wbar')\Bigr] + \wbar J(\wbar),
$$ (eq-g16)

which is never used to *produce* a limit — only to *evaluate* one after it exists.

### 3.3 The two core lemmas (the entire engine — 76 lines in the source)

**Lemma γ2 (boundedness).** $W_1$ is bounded on the compactified domain. *Idea*: iterate the
sup of [](#eq-gamma-up) inward shell by shell; with $\lambda(1) = 1/(\Rcal\ThornG) < 1$ and bounded
forcing, the sup recursion $M_{n+1} \leq \lambda(1+\delta_n)M_n + \bar{F}(1+\delta_n)$ with
$\sum\delta_n < \infty$ is trapped. Self-contained — Theorem A1 is *not* imported.

**Lemma γ3 (boundary stability — the engine).** For two-sided shell recursions
$W_{n+1} \lessgtr \lambda_n W_n + F_n$ with $\lambda_n \to \lambda < 1$ and $F_n \to
F_\infty$: both $\limsup$ and $\liminf$ of $W_n$ equal $F_\infty/(1-\lambda)$.
*Proof (the four lines):* let $M := \limsup M_n$; passing to the limsup in the sup-recursion
gives $M \leq \lambda M + F_\infty$, so $M \leq F_\infty/(1-\lambda)$; the mirrored inf
recursion gives $m \geq F_\infty/(1-\lambda)$; $m \leq M$ closes it. $\blacksquare$

### 3.4 Theorem I (the owner's one number): the boundary value at $\qup > 1$

:::{prf:theorem} Theorem I (= Theorem γ-A): continuity at the boundary; the scalar boundary equation
:label: thm-I
Assume (A1)–(A6) and $\qup > 1$ ($\iff \Rcal\ThornG > 1$). Then $W_1(z) = \wbar\,g(\wbar)$
extends to a continuous function on the closed interval $[0, \bar{z}]$, and its boundary
value solves the scalar equation

$$
W(0) = \lambda\, W(0) + F(0), \qquad \lambda = \frac{1}{\Rcal\ThornG}, \quad F(0) = \frac{c_J}{\Rcal},
$$ (eq-g31)

whose unique solution is

$$
W(0) = \frac{F(0)}{1-\lambda} = \frac{c_J\,\ThornG}{\Rcal\ThornG - 1}
= B = \frac{\kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2}{2(\Rcal\ThornG - 1)}.
$$ (eq-boundary-value)

Equivalently $g(\wbar) = B/\wbar + o(1/\wbar)$.
:::

*Proof (assembled).* γ2 gives boundedness; γ0/γ1 put the shell sequences in γ3's hypotheses
($\lambda_n \to \lambda < 1$ by boundary continuity, $F_n \to F(0)$ by [](#eq-54), the
$(1 \pm \hat{D}/\wbar)$ factors are the $\delta_n$ with $\sum \delta_n < \infty$ because
$\sum 1/\wbar_n$ is geometric); γ3 forces $\limsup = \liminf = F(0)/(1-\lambda)$. Only *then* is
[](#eq-g16) passed to the limit (dominated convergence), which yields [](#eq-g31) — the order
matters, and is what makes the argument non-circular (refuter-verified explicitly).
$\blacksquare$

This is the promised punchline: **at the boundary point "wealth $= \infty$" the entire Euler
equation degenerates to one linear scalar equation, and the finite number representing the
exact limit is its unique solution.** The unrolled form $B = \sum_{k \geq 0} \lambda^k F(0)$
is the geometric resolvent — the same number every other proof in the program computes by its
own route (§8). [](#fig-boundary) shows both faces: panel (a) the **GIC-cap calibration
CAL-CCAP** ($\qup = 1.474$, Stage B), where the boundary value $B_\psi = 357$ exists and
the boundary equation pins it — but the approach outruns reachable wealth ($W/B_\psi = 0.42$
at the top of the estimation grid, $0.91$ even at $130\times$ the grid top; the knife-edge
window of §7); panel (b) a labeled illustration calibration where the same convergence
completes in-window (match 1.3%; inset slope $-0.598$ vs $-0.6$).

*The Gordon-growth reading of $B$.* Along the descent, the premium "dividend" grows: each
quarter wealth shrinks by the factor $\ThornG$, so the premium $\propto 1/\wbar$ grows by the
factor $1/\ThornG$. Discounting per quarter is $1/\Rcal$. The value of a growing perpetuity
with dividend growth $1/\ThornG$ and discount $\Rcal$ converges exactly when
$\Rcal\ThornG > 1$ — the Gordon condition "discount rate exceeds growth rate", which *is*
$\qup > 1$ — and then $B = \text{(first dividend)}/(\text{discount} - \text{growth})$,
concretely $B = c_J\ThornG/(\Rcal\ThornG - 1)$: the exact closed form of Theorem I.
Economists can read the entire theorem as: *the precautionary shortfall of the wealthy is
the Gordon value of their future precautionary premia.*

(thm-II)=
## 4. Theorem II: the trichotomy — three things a linear recursion can do at a boundary

The compensation dial makes the multiplier a function of $\qtilde$:
$\lambda(\qtilde) = \ThornG^{\qup-\qtilde}$ (general $\psi$:
$\lambda_B(\qtilde) = e^{\Lfun(\qtilde)}$ — so "boundary multiplier $= 1$" is *literally*
Lemma A5's eigen-equation [](#eq-E)). Everything in the theorem is the elementary
classification of the shell recursion by $(\lambda, \lim F_n)$:

| regime | multiplier | forcing | boundary behavior | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\qup > 1$, $\qtilde=1$ | $\lambda < 1$ | $F_n \to c_J/\Rcal > 0$ | **contracting fixed point** — one number | Theorem I: $W(0) = B$ |
| $\qup = 1$, $\qtilde=1$ | $\lambda = 1$ | $F_n \to c_J/\Rcal > 0$ | **linear escape** — a slope, not a value | Theorem γ-R |
| $\qup < 1$, $\qtilde=\qup$ | $\lambda = 1$ | $F_n \to 0$ geometrically | **multiplier exactly 1, fading forcing** — a limit *set* | γ-C |

In Gordon-growth language: the three rows are the **convergent perpetuity** (discount beats
dividend growth — a finite value, priced off near dividends), the **$r = g$ knife-edge**
(value accrues linearly in the horizon — here the horizon is $\ln \wbar/\Lambda$, hence the
$\ln \wbar$ law), and the **divergent case** (dividend growth beats discounting — the "value" is
dominated by the latest, largest dividends, i.e. by the journey's end near the target, whose
imprint is the periodic fine structure).

(thm-gR)=
**Resonance (Theorem γ-R; PROVEN).** At $\qup = 1$ ($\Rcal\ThornG = 1$), the shell
recursion is $W_{n+1} = W_n(1 + O(\delta_n)) + F_n$ with $F_n \to c_J/\Rcal$: by the perturbed
Stolz–Cesàro lemma (γ-R1), $W_n/n \to c_J/\Rcal$; converting shells to wealth
($n(\wbar) = \ln(\wbar/\wbar_a)/\Lambda$),

$$
\frac{\wbar}{\ln \wbar}\, g(\wbar) \;\to\; \frac{c_J}{\Rcal\,\Lambda} = \frac{\kap(\rho+1)\sigma^2}{2\Lambda}
$$ (eq-resonance-limit)

— the sharp resonance constant of Theorem B-res's $\psi\equiv1$ corollary, re-proven in a few
lines (measured to 0.07% in the source suite). [](#fig-trichotomy)(b) exhibits it at the
**resonance patience level of the College fundamentals** ($\beta$ root-found so
$\qup = 1.000000$ exactly): $W_n$ grows linearly in the tilted shell index with per-step
increment $\to c_J^B/\Rcal$ (deepest measured increment 1.101 vs theory 1.113). *This is a
knife-edge, not a neighborhood*: the theorem is a fixed-$\qup$ statement, and the crossover
to it is non-uniform on any window with $|\qup - 1| \ln \wbar = O(1)$.

(thm-gC)=
**$\qup < 1$: the boundary is a circle, not a point (γ-C; envelope PROVEN, sharp form
imported).** At $\qtilde = \qup$ the map $z \mapsto z/\ThornG$ shifts log wealth by exactly
$\Lambda$, so it leaves unchanged the *position within the multiplicative cycle*: the
fractional part $\varphi = \{\ln \wbar/\Lambda\}$. In log wealth the dynamics repeat with
period $\Lambda$, so what matters asymptotically is the position modulo $\Lambda$ — a circle
of circumference $\Lambda$ rather than a single boundary point. Wealth levels sharing the
same position in the cycle (equal log-wealth modulo $\Lambda$) form a family that the shell
recursion maps to itself; on each such family the recursion neither contracts nor expands
(its multiplier is exactly 1) while its forcing fades geometrically. Proven self-contained
here: the two-sided envelope converges to a bounded positive limit set (γ-C1), and the
period-by-period smoothing of the position-modulo-$\Lambda$ distribution contributed by the
continuous part of the shock is geometrically summable (γ-C2). Upgrading to convergence
separately at each position of the cycle needs a uniform bound on how much the limit can
vary under small shifts of the position (a modulus of continuity in the cycle variable) —
**GAP-γ-equicont**, honestly open *for this engine*; the sharp statement —
$\wbar^{\qup} g(\wbar) = P(\{\ln \wbar/\Lambda\}) + o(1)$, with $P$ the log-periodic
factor — is Theorem A2 of the original chain, imported. [](#fig-trichotomy)(c) makes the
practical point sharply: at **CAL-HS's estimated patience** ($\Lambda = 0.0125$) the
periodic component provably has no effect at any attainable precision: its total amplitude —
the oscillation $\operatorname{osc}(P) := \sup P - \inf P$ of the log-periodic factor —
satisfies $\operatorname{osc}(P)/\bar{P} \sim e^{-\alpha/\Lambda}$, far below any numerical
floor. That is, *the boundary circle collapses numerically to a point* — which is exactly
why a constant-coefficient power-law extrapolant works in the HAFiscal/HARK code; the inset
shows the one calibration in the program with a numerically resolved tone ($\Lambda = 0.69$,
amplitude $\approx 8\cdot10^{-4}$, correlation 0.9999 between the measured wiggle and its
predicted position in the cycle) — the honest evidence that the circle is real.

## 5. Theorem III: permanent shocks — the human-wealth-revaluation channel

With permanent shocks the exact recursion becomes the **master identity**

$$
\psi'\, \wbar' = \ThornG\, \wbar + W' + \Rcal\, g(\wbar), \qquad W := \psi(\theta + h - 1) - h, \quad \E[W] = 0,
$$ (eq-B31)

$$
\sigma_B^2 := \operatorname{Var}(W) = \E[\psi^2]\,\sigma_\theta^2 + h^2\,\sigma_\psi^2.
$$ (eq-sigmaB)

(Watch the typographical neighbors: lowercase $\wbar$ is total wealth, capital $W'$ the
mean-zero income-revaluation shock — and neither is the compensated gap $W_{\qtilde}(\cdot)$
of §3.) The $h^2\sigma_\psi^2$ term is the **human-wealth revaluation channel**: a permanent
shock reprices the entire future income stream — all $h$ units of it — so permanent-shock
models sit *further* from the exponential heuristic than transitory-only ones. The
$\psi$-weighted one-step relation makes the compactified multiplier
$\lambda_B = \E[\psi^2]/(\Rcal\ThornG) = e^{\Lfun(1)} < 1 \iff \qup > 1$, and the identical
γ2/γ3 pattern (boundedness via the stopped expectation-unroll Lemma γ-B2, which covers the
*general* bounded-$\psi$ case including the empirically relevant $\psi_{\min} \leq \ThornG$;
key identity $\E[\psi^2 (\ThornG/\psi)] = \ThornG$) delivers:

:::{prf:theorem} Theorem III (= Theorem γ-B): the Stage-B boundary value
:label: thm-III
At $\qup > 1$,

$$
\wbar\, g(\wbar) \;\to\; B_\psi = \frac{\kap(\rho+1)\sigma_B^2}{2(\Rcal\ThornG - \E[\psi^2])}
$$ (eq-Bpsi-boundary)
(measured to 0.22%/0.35% in two adversarial $\psi$-designs). The amplitude is
$\psi$-skew-blind: third moments enter only one order deeper.
:::

Stage-B resonance and $\qup < 1$ are OPEN *for this engine* (the shell index becomes a
random walk whose occupation control is exactly the original chain's tilt toolkit); the sharp
results are Theorems B-res, B2, B2-arith of `stage_B_proof.md`, imported.

## 6. The economics of the proof: the journey to the target

(def-return-region)=
The path-sum reading (route α) supplies the economic narrative for the same numbers. Iterate
[](#eq-five-up)–[](#eq-five-dn) forward: the gap at wealth $\wbar$ is the **expected present
discounted value of the Kimball precautionary premia the household will collect along its
buffer-stock journey** from $\wbar$ down toward **the return region around the target
$\hat{m}$** — the precise object behind the phrase "toward the target". In BST's language
the destination is the individual target $\hat{m}$ (unique under WRIC + FVAC + GIC-Mod
per [BST's buffer-stock-target theorem](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#thm-target),
and computable from [BST's implicit equation](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#eq-mtargimplicit)
$(\hat{m} - c(\hat{m}))\,\Rcal\,\E[\psi^{-1}] + 1 = \hat{m}$) or the pseudo-target
$\check{m}$ ([GIC-Raw](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#thm-mssbalexists)).
What the unrolled argument itself consumes is only this: **a compact wealth interval
containing the target, on which the descent's bookkeeping can stop.** Concretely (Stage A),
take the wealth levels $\wbar \leq \zeta$ with $\zeta := C_0/(1-\ThornG)$ — §3.2's
enlargement constant: above $\zeta$ the recursion [](#eq-31) moves strictly down almost
surely ($\wbar' \leq \ThornG\wbar + C_0 < \wbar$), from inside the interval wealth cannot
exit upward ($\ThornG\zeta + C_0 = \zeta$), the descent from any $\wbar$ enters it in
$\approx \ln(\wbar/\zeta)/\Lambda$ quarters, and the target must lie inside (above $\zeta$
wealth falls almost surely, contradicting $\E[m'/m] = 1$ at $\hat{m}$). The region is
compact **because the transitory shock has bounded support** — part of the maintained
income-process assumption (BST,
[Assumption *ass-shocks*](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#ass-shocks);
[§2](#sec-model)): bounded $\theta$, with the sandwich $g \leq \bar{g}$, is what makes the
one-step displacement bound $C_0$ — hence the upper edge $\zeta$ — finite and computable
from the primitives ($C_0 = \max(1,\, \theta_{\max} - 1 + \Rcal\bar{g})$ works, read off
[](#eq-31)). With *unbounded* transitory shocks the region is not compact — a single large
draw passes any point, no finite $C_0$ exists — and the renewal argument as given needs the
bound; the decay theorem itself still holds via the L4′ moment extension, which replaces the
compact-interval bookkeeping with moment bounds ([](#sec-dualroot), item 3). With permanent
shocks the interval is entered and re-entered rather than absorbing ([](#sec-dualroot),
item 2 quantifies the excursions above it). The journey picture is exactly Carroll's (1997)
narrative made quantitative. The trichotomy is then *where the gap is earned*
([](#fig-journey)):

- $\qup > 1$: **the first rungs dominate** — the gap is priced at the start of the journey,
  hence the clean $B/\wbar$ with the geometric resolvent $1/(\Rcal\ThornG - 1)$.
- $\qup = 1$: every rung contributes equally — $T(\wbar) \approx \ln \wbar/\Lambda$ equal
  premia $\Rightarrow$ the $\ln \wbar$ law.
- $\qup < 1$: **the journey's end dominates** — the gap is priced near the target, which is
  why its fine structure (the periodic factor's phase) remembers the landing position
  $\{\ln \wbar/\Lambda\}$.

Measured on the estimated HAFiscal calibrations (the bookkeeping is the $\psi^2$-tilted Wald chain —
step $\Lfun'(1)$, weight $\lambda_B$ per rung — which the figure build verified is the *only*
unroll-consistent accounting at estimated patience): the journey's-end/boundary share is
**89%** for CAL-HS ($\qup = 0.38$, end-dominated), **50%** for CAL-CTOP ($\qup = 0.69$,
the transition), and **2%** for the GIC-cap ceiling CAL-CCAP ($\qup = 1.47$,
head-dominated) — with all three per-rung weights $\lambda_B$ within 1% of 1: **estimated
patience sits close to the resonance knife-edge**, which is the deep reason the historical
exponential top-of-grid heuristic was locally harmless even though it is asymptotically
impossible ([Proposition A0](#prop-imp-A0), in the appendix).

behavior is delicate at realistic patience: the value of the shortfall is spread almost
evenly over the whole journey, so no part of the wealth domain is safely negligible.

(sec-dualroot)=
### 6.1 How rarely is the tail visited? The dual (Kesten) root, for economists

The theorem describes $c(m)$ at wealth levels the agent rarely — in one important case never —
holds. This subsection quantifies "rarely", because the correct quantifier is itself an object
worth knowing: the tail exponent of a **Kesten process**. (Evidence for every number below:
`verify_dualroot_checks.py`, ALL PASS, same 7-atom $\psi$ discretization as the figures.)

Economists have met this machine under another name: **random growth**. Multiply a variable
by an i.i.d. factor each period (plus a small additive perturbation); if the factor shrinks
*on average* but *sometimes exceeds one*, the stationary distribution develops a Pareto
upper tail whose exponent solves $\E[\text{factor}^\zeta] = 1$. This is the mechanism behind
Zipf's law for cities — Gabaix (1999) shows that Gibrat-style proportional random growth of
city populations, with a small friction keeping cities from vanishing, produces a power-law
size distribution, with the Zipf exponent $1$ emerging in the small-friction limit — and the
standard account of Pareto tails in wealth and firm size. Gabaix (2009) surveys both the
empirical catalog (income and wealth, cities, firms, stock returns, trading volume,
executive pay) and the generating theory, including precisely the exponent equation
$\E[A^\zeta] = 1$ below; Gabaix (2016) is the introduction written for the general
economist, arguing these are true quantitative laws — empirically established *and*
theoretically understood. Everything below is that familiar machine, applied to a single
household's normalized wealth rather than to a cross-section of cities or firms.


Rearranging the master identity [](#eq-B31), normalized wealth follows

$$
\wbar' = A'\, \wbar + B', \qquad A' := \frac{\ThornG}{\psi'}, \qquad
B' := \frac{W' + \Rcal\, g(\wbar)}{\psi'} \ \ (\text{bounded: } g \leq \bar{g}),
$$ (eq-kesten)

a stochastic recursion with i.i.d. multiplier — the workhorse of the wealth-distribution
literature (Kesten 1973; Goldie 1991; econ packagings Benhabib–Bisin–Zhu 2011, Toda 2014; the
Gouin-Bonenfant–Toda 2023 toolkit's `getZeta` computes exactly the object below). The **dual
(Kesten) root** is the unique $\zeta^* > 0$ solving

$$
\E[A^{\zeta^*}] \;=\; \E\Bigl[\bigl(\ThornG/\psi\bigr)^{\zeta^*}\Bigr] \;=\; 1,
$$ (eq-dualroot)

which exists precisely when the multiplier (i) contracts on average, $\E[\ln A] < 0$, and
(ii) occasionally expands, $\Pr(A > 1) > 0$. When it exists, the stationary distribution of
$\wbar$ has a power tail with exponent $\zeta^*$: $\Pr(\wbar > t)$ decays essentially like
$t^{-\zeta^*}$ (the upper bound is an elementary Chernoff/supermartingale estimate; the
matching lower bound is the Kesten–Goldie theorem, with the usual non-arithmeticity caveat —
a discretized $\psi$ technically sits in the lattice case, where Kevei's 2017 version
supplies the log-periodic analogue). **Duality made precise:** $\zeta^*$ and the theorem's
$\qup$ solve the two members of one Mellin family — the *dual* equation [](#eq-dualroot)
governs the tail of the wealth *distribution*; the *primal* equation [](#eq-E) governs the
approach of the consumption *function*. §1.2's obstruction is the case where the dual root
fails to exist while the primal always does.

**The reachability taxonomy** (answering: with random shocks, what can "unreachable" even
mean?):

1. **$\psi \equiv 1$, bounded $\theta$ — literally unreachable.** $A \equiv \ThornG < 1$: no
   expansion, ever; $\wbar' \leq \ThornG \wbar + C_0$ gives an absorbing interval (the
   [return region of §6](#def-return-region)), so the ergodic support is **compact** — zero
   probability beyond a finite point, not merely small. This is the strongest form of §1.2's
   contrast: no wealth tail of any kind, yet the gap is exactly a power law.
2. **Permanent shocks, even bounded (the HAFiscal case) — reachable but power-law rare.**
   The 7-atom estimated $\psi$ has $\psi_{\min} = 0.9217 < \ThornG$ at *all three*
   calibrations, so $\Pr(A > 1) > 0$: a lucky run of small permanent shocks ratchets
   normalized wealth up without bound — *the support is unbounded even though every shock is
   bounded*. "Behaviorally reachable" is then a measure-and-time statement: tail mass
   $\sim t^{-\zeta^*}$, excursions mean-reverting at rate $|\E[\ln A]|$ per quarter.

   | calibration | $\E[\ln A]$ | dual root $\zeta^*$ | $\Pr(\wbar > 10\times\text{typical})$ (Chernoff scale) |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | CAL-HS | $-0.0113$ | **9.19** | $\sim 6\cdot10^{-10}$ |
   | CAL-CTOP | $-0.0039$ | **3.17** | $\sim 7\cdot10^{-4}$ |
   | CAL-CCAP (GIC-cap ceiling) | $\mathbf{+0.0009}$ | none | no stationary $\wbar$ from impatience alone |

   Patience fattens the tail ($9.2 \to 3.2 \to$ none) — the familiar patience-to-Pareto
   mechanism of the wealth-distribution literature, here seen from inside a single agent's
   problem.
3. **Unbounded $\theta$ — any level is possible.** One large transitory draw passes any
   point, so there is no finite maximal wealth at all; the ergodic tail then *inherits the
   income tail* (Stachurski–Toda 2019). The theorem is indifferent: the L4′ extension covers
   unbounded $\theta$.

**The patience-ceiling exception.** At the GIC-cap ceiling (CAL-CCAP) the mean log-drift is
*positive*: under the reduced i.i.d. dynamics that calibration's normalized wealth drifts up
forever — impatience alone never pulls it back, and no dual root exists. The cap's
construction pins *raw* growth impatience to hold — barely ($\ThornG = 0.99964 < 1$) — while
the $\psi$-modified conditions fail ($\E[\ln(\ThornG/\psi)] = +0.0009 > 0$ and
$\E[\ThornG/\psi] \approx 1.002 > 1$): the raw, log-drift, and expectation-of-ratio
conditions are separated by Jensen gaps, and the ceiling calibration sits between raw and
modified — an estimated-parameters instance of
[BST's condition-lattice distinctions](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#factors-defined-and-compared),
the case in which GIC-Mod fails while GIC holds, so only the
[pseudo-target](https://llorracc.github.io/BufferStockTheory-Latest/#thm-mssbalexists) survives.
What truncates that calibration's ergodic wealth in the full model (the distribution whose
extreme upper quantile fixes the top of the estimation grid) is **mortality with
replacement** — the reset mechanism of the wealth-tail literature (Toda 2014) — and within an
expected working life its upward drift moves wealth only $\approx 15\%$.

**Practical conclusion, sharpened.** The asymptotic region is visited with stationary
probability ranging from $t^{-9}$ (CAL-HS) down to "only via the mortality-reset tail"
(CAL-CCAP) — and in the $\psi \equiv 1$ benchmark, never. The theorem's practical role is
therefore exactly as §7 frames it: a boundary condition and extrapolation form for the
*function*, not a description of states to simulate.

## 7. The computational payoff: why the compactified core is the right presentation

1. **The boundary value is a boundary condition.** Solving on a compactified grid
   $z \in [0, \bar{z}]$ (equivalently, a top-of-grid asymptotic condition) is standard
   numerical practice; Theorem I upgrades the practice to principle: the *exact* boundary
   data at $z = 0$ is $W(0) = B$ ($\qup > 1$), i.e. $c(m) \approx \kap(m-1+h) - B/m$ at
   the top of the grid — with $B$ known in closed form from the calibration.
2. **The extrapolation form of record.** The gap extrapolant
   $g \approx C\,\wbar^{-q} = C\,(m-1+h)^{-q}$,
   $q = \min(1, \qup)$, is the asymptotically correct form — the theorem behind the
   `decay_extrap='powerlaw'` flip in the HAFiscal/HARK code (RECONCILED-002; HARK PR
   `fix-aggshock-pf-decay-extrap`), replacing the historical exponential heuristic, which
   the appendix ([Proposition A0](#prop-imp-A0)) shows is not merely inaccurate but
   *impossible* as an asymptotic form.
3. **The knife-edge window, quantified on HAFiscal's own numbers.** At the estimated
   calibrations the growth patience factor is close to 1 ($\Lambda \to 0$) and the asymptotic
   regime begins only at astronomically large wealth. CAL-CCAP makes this concrete:
   $\Lambda = 3.6\cdot10^{-4}$, $\qup = 1.474$, $B_\psi = 357$ — and the compensated gap
   has covered only **42%** of the way to its boundary value at the top of the estimation
   grid, 91% even at $130\times$ the grid top ([](#fig-boundary)a). That is *why*
   the exponential heuristic was locally harmless on historical grids ($\leq 0.22\%$ policy
   effect at the audited window) even though it is asymptotically wrong.
4. **A built-in diagnostic.** Theorem γ-T's detection property is a practical grid test:
   compensate the measured gap by trial exponents; only $\min(1, \qup)$ stabilizes.
5. **The computer-science face.** In algorithmic terms the sandwich
   [](#eq-five-up)–[](#eq-five-dn) *is* a perturbed divide-and-conquer recurrence, and
   [](#eq-E) is its Akra–Bazzi critical-exponent equation $\sum_i a_i b_i^{p} = 1$ at
   $p = -\qup$; the trichotomy including the resonance log is the master theorem's three
   cases (Akra–Bazzi 1998; Leighton 1996). That proof is carried out in full in
   `alt_proof_econcs.md`, with hypothesis-match tables against the machine-formalized
   statements (Isabelle AFP `Akra_Bazzi`; Lean 4 mathlib4 `Computability/AkraBazzi`) and
   executed interval-arithmetic certificates.
